# Scholarly Literature Review: Prosumption, Value Co-Creation, and Sustainable Collaborative Consumption

## Overview
This document provides an annotated synthesis of 16 peer-reviewed academic foundations informing the theoretical framing, index methodology, and empirical interpretation of this study. The research integrates three core theoretical lenses:
1. **Prosumption & Value Co-Creation Theory** (Service-Dominant Logic, consumer agency, dual-role market participation).
2. **Theory of Planned Behavior & Decision-Making** (Attitudes, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and behavioral intentions).
3. **Multi-Dimensional Perceived Value & Motivation Theory** (Trade-offs between economic self-interest, functional convenience, social affiliation, and environmental sustainability).

---

## 1. Foundational Prosumption & Value Co-Creation Literature

### 1. Xie, C., Bagozzi, R. P., & Troye, S. V. (2008)
- **Title**: *Trying to prosume: toward a theory of consumers as co-creators of value*
- **Journal**: *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, 36(1), 109–122.
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s11747-007-0060-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-007-0060-2)
- **Core Contribution**: Grounded in the "theory of trying," this paper conceptualizes prosumption as an active behavioral process where consumers expend effort, skills, and time to co-produce goods and services. It establishes that prosumption is not merely an economic decision but a goal-directed activity shaped by self-efficacy and perceived control.
- **Application to our Project**: Informs our definition of prosumers as dual actors who transition from passive receivers of utility into active value co-creators, directly justifying the inclusion of provider frequency and sector breadth in our Prosumer Engagement Index (PEI).

### 2. Vargo, S. L., & Lusch, R. F. (2004, 2008)
- **Title**: *Evolving to a New Dominant Logic for Marketing* / *Service-dominant logic: continuing the evolution*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Marketing*, 68(1), 1–17 / *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, 36(1), 1–10.
- **DOI**: [10.1509/jmkg.68.1.1.24036](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.68.1.1.24036) / [10.1007/s11747-007-0069-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-007-0069-6)
- **Core Contribution**: Introduces Service-Dominant (S-D) Logic, shifting marketing thought from goods-centered exchange to service-centered exchange. S-D Logic posits that value is always collaboratively co-created through the integration of operant resources (knowledge, skills, and digital tools) between market actors.
- **Application to our Project**: Serves as the overarching macro-marketing paradigm. Digital sharing platforms are conceptualized as resource-integrating ecosystems where consumers and providers co-create mutual value.

### 3. Ritzer, G., & Jurgenson, N. (2010)
- **Title**: *Production, Consumption, Prosumption: The nature of capitalism in the age of the digital 'prosumer'*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Consumer Culture*, 10(1), 13–36.
- **DOI**: [10.1177/1469540509354673](https://doi.org/10.1177/1469540509354673)
- **Core Contribution**: Traces the evolution of prosumption from Toffler's (1980) original conception to the Web 2.0 and platform economy. Argues that digital networks dramatically lower the marginal cost of production and communication, turning everyday consumers into "prosumers on platforms."
- **Application to our Project**: Provides the historical and structural foundation for why digital platforms (ride-hailing, home-sharing, peer freelance) have scaled prosumption into a pervasive economic phenomenon.

### 4. Toffler, A. (1980)
- **Title**: *The Third Wave*
- **Publisher**: Bantam Books, New York.
- **Core Contribution**: Originally coined the portmanteau "prosumer" to describe the blending of producer and consumer roles, predicting that post-industrial society would dismantle the rigid division of labor created during the Industrial Revolution.
- **Application to our Project**: Foundational conceptual touchstone defining the baseline transition from industrial consumerism to collaborative participation.

## 2. Sharing Economy Motivations & Consumer Behavior

### 5. Hamari, J., Sjöklint, M., & Ukkonen, A. (2016)
- **Title**: *The sharing economy: Why people participate in collaborative consumption*
- **Journal**: *Journal of the Association for Information Science and Technology*, 67(9), 2047–2059.
- **DOI**: [10.1002/asi.23552](https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.23552)
- **Core Contribution**: Conducted a pioneering structural equation modeling study examining motivations in collaborative consumption. Discovered an **attitude-behavior gap**: while environmental sustainability drives positive *attitudes* toward sharing, *actual behavioral participation* is overwhelmingly driven by utilitarian and economic benefits (cost savings).
- **Application to our Project**: Serves as the benchmark for our motivational analysis (`q4` advantages vs. `q11` provider motives), guiding our finding that economic savings drive general adoption while sustainability motives differentiate high-engagement prosumers.

### 6. Benoit, S., Baker, T. L., Bolton, R. N., Gruber, T., & Kandampully, J. (2017)
- **Title**: *A triadic framework for collaborative consumption (CC): Motives, capabilities and challenges*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Business Research*, 79, 219–227.
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jbusres.2017.05.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2017.05.002)
- **Core Contribution**: Formulates the canonical triadic architecture of collaborative markets: (1) Platform Provider (intermediary), (2) Peer Service Provider, and (3) Customer. Analyzes the distinctive capabilities, motives, and governance mechanisms governing peer-to-peer exchanges.
- **Application to our Project**: Structures our analytical distinction between Consumer-Only, Provider-Only, and Prosumer roles, demonstrating how dual actors operate across both peer legs of the triad.

### 7. Bardhi, F., & Eckhardt, G. M. (2012)
- **Title**: *Access-based consumption: The nature of power and division between consumers and providers*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Consumer Research*, 39(4), 881–898.
- **DOI**: [10.1086/666616](https://doi.org/10.1086/666616)
- **Core Contribution**: Conceptualizes "access-based consumption" where consumers acquire temporary access to goods (e.g. car sharing) without transfer of legal ownership. Finds that access consumption is characterized by self-interest and utilitarian convenience rather than romanticized community sharing.
- **Application to our Project**: Guides the interpretation of market substitution (`q3`) and functional convenience (`q4.3`), contextualizing why consumers replace traditional ownership with platform access.

### 8. Belk, R. (2014)
- **Title**: *You are what you can access: Sharing and collaborative consumption online*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Business Research*, 67(8), 1595–1600.
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jbusres.2013.10.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2013.10.001)
- **Core Contribution**: Clarifies the distinction between true non-monetary sharing (distributive sharing) and "pseudo-sharing" (commercial collaborative consumption mediated for fees).
- **Application to our Project**: Informs our analysis of non-monetary exchange (`adv_exchange` / `q4.6`) versus monetary supplementary earnings (`q11.1`, `q11.2`), distinguishing community prosumers from commercial micro-providers.

## 3. Behavioral Measurement & Formative Scale Methodology

### 9. Paik, N., & Rhee, K. (2009)
- **Title**: *The Conceptualization and the Development of Prosumer Propensity Scale*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Consumer Studies*, 20(3), 135–161.
- **Core Contribution**: Developed a multi-item psychometric scale capturing prosumer psychological propensity across information search, peer advocacy, product customization, and participatory orientation.
- **Application to our Project**: Serves as a key methodological reference point. As noted in our research design, while Paik & Rhee developed a reflective psychological propensity scale, our study constructs a **formative behavioural index (PEI)** based on observed real-world market actions.

### 10. Diamantopoulos, A., & Winklhofer, H. M. (2001)
- **Title**: *Index construction with formative indicators: An alternative to scale development*
- **Journal**: *Journal of Marketing Research*, 38(2), 269–277.
- **DOI**: [10.1509/jmkr.38.2.269.18845](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.38.2.269.18845)
- **Core Contribution**: Foundational methodological paper on formative index construction. Demonstrates that when indicators form or cause the composite construct (rather than being caused by a single latent trait), traditional reliability checks like Cronbach's alpha and exploratory factor analysis are conceptually inappropriate; researchers must establish content validity, indicator multicollinearity, and external criterion validity.
- **Application to our Project**: Direct methodological warrant for constructing the Prosumer Engagement Index (PEI) as a weighted additive combination of frequency, breadth, and market substitution, validated via criterion-related correlations.

## 4. Empirical Studies on Eurobarometer 467 & Prosumer Governance

### 11. Management Decision (2026)
- **Title**: *Do prosumers behave differently from other consumers on collaborative consumption platforms?*
- **Journal**: *Management Decision*, Ahead-of-print.
- **DOI**: [10.1108/MD-04-2023-0664](https://doi.org/10.1108/MD-04-2023-0664)
- **Core Contribution**: Utilizes Flash Eurobarometer 467 to contrast consumers and prosumers using PLS-SEM and fsQCA, demonstrating that dual-role prosumers have higher recommendation intentions and higher intention to offer future services.
- **Critical Differentiation in our Project**: While this 2026 paper examines binary consumer vs. prosumer classifications, our project develops a **continuous, multi-dimensional behavioural index (PEI)**, conducts **K-Means segmentation into 4 distinct personas**, and analyzes the **sustainability/resource-efficiency dimension** (`q11.8`), delivering unique marketing strategy insights.

### 12. Kelleci, R., & Eşsiz, O. (2026)
- **Title**: *Prosumers and Sustainable Market Governance: Development of the Community-Oriented Marketing Approach Scale*
- **Journal**: *Business Strategy and the Environment*, 35(2), 845–863.
- **DOI**: [10.1002/bse.71118](https://doi.org/10.1002/bse.71118)
- **Core Contribution**: Develops a scale measuring community-oriented marketing approaches, showing how prosumers foster sustainable market governance through shared value creation, community ownership, and participatory power.
- **Application to our Project**: Strengthens our discussion of platform governance, demonstrating how platforms can institutionalize prosumer participation to advance circular resource recirculation.

### 13. Andreotti, A., Anselmi, G., Eichhorn, T., Hoffmann, C. P., & Micheli, M. (2021)
- **Title**: *Participation in the sharing economy: Comparing providers and consumers in Europe*
- **Journal**: *Cambridge Journal of Regions, Economy and Society*, 14(3), 485–503.
- **DOI**: [10.1093/cjres/rsaa038](https://doi.org/10.1093/cjres/rsaa038)
- **Core Contribution**: Examined Flash Eurobarometer 467 microdata to model the determinants of becoming a provider versus consumer across Europe. Found that providers are more educated, younger, and face distinct institutional frictions regarding taxation and employment status.
- **Application to our Project**: Empirically validates our Model 1 logistic regression specification and informs our barrier analysis (`q8`, `q12`).

### 14. Frenken, K., & Schor, J. (2017)
- **Title**: *Putting the sharing economy into perspective*
- **Journal**: *Environmental Innovation and Societal Transitions*, 23, 3–10.
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.eist.2017.01.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eist.2017.01.003)
- **Core Contribution**: Distinguishes sharing economy activities into four quadrants based on temporary access vs. ownership transfer and peer-to-peer vs. business-to-consumer. Highlights the environmental potential of idle capacity utilization.
- **Application to our Project**: Direct foundation for our Sustainability Discussion (Phase 16), explaining how prosumer asset-sharing extends product lifetimes and recirculates physical capital.

### 15. Geissinger, A., Laurell, C., Öberg, C., & Sandström, C. (2019)
- **Title**: *How sustainable is the sharing economy? On the sustainability connotations of sharing economy platforms*
- **Journal**: *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*, 141, 19–29.
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.techfore.2018.06.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2018.06.019)
- **Core Contribution**: Analyzes social media communication and consumer perceptions of sharing platforms, discovering that while platforms market themselves around green sustainability, consumers prioritize economic and social convenience.
- **Application to our Project**: Provides the scholarly context for our Persona 3 (Sustainability Co-Creators) vs. Persona 1/2 (Frugal/Convenience Users) segmentation findings.

### 16. Ajzen, I. (1991)
- **Title**: *The Theory of Planned Behavior*
- **Journal**: *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 50(2), 179–211.
- **DOI**: [10.1016/0749-5978(91)90020-T](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(91)90020-T)
- **Core Contribution**: Canonical psychological framework linking attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control to behavioral intentions and actions.
- **Application to our Project**: Used as a conceptual lens to explain the cognitive translation of perceived advantages into recommendation advocacy (`q6`) and prospective offering intentions (`q7`).
